Before you start Step 1 of 2 · Preparation

What you are judging

You will see a figure from a research paper on the left, and an animated presentation of that same figure on the right. Your job is to say how well the animation presents the figure.

There are no right answers, and you are not expected to know the paper. Judge what you can see.

How to watch

  • Slider is how you move through the animation: drag it, or use the ← and → arrow keys to step a frame at a time.
  • The questions appear once you have taken the slider all the way to the end. Until then they stay hidden.
  • Video plays it through automatically instead, at half speed by default.
  • Go back and forth as much as you like — nothing is one-shot.
  • Some animations start almost blank and build up. That is the style, not a fault — but whether it works is exactly the sort of thing worth rating.

What the questions mean

  • Coverage — does the animation show what the figure shows? Missing boxes, invented arrows, wrong positions, unreadable labels.
  • Quality — does it highlight sensible things in a sensible order, at a comfortable pace?
  • Style — each animation is supposed to follow one named style. You are told which; say whether it does.
  • Narration (later sections) — does the text make sense on its own, and does it match what is on screen when it appears?
You can change any answer before submitting a trial. Use Previous and Next, or click a chip in the strip above the question. People often revise early answers once they have seen the whole animation — that is expected, not cheating.

A quick check

Answer these the way you would in the real study. It is a check that the task is clear, not a test of the subject matter.
